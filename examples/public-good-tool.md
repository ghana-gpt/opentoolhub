# Public-good tool example

## Product idea

A local civic product that helps residents find public services, community updates, and useful places without searching across several city websites.

## Problem it solves

Public information is often split between departments, notice boards, and social feeds. Residents need one clear place to understand what is available and what has changed.

## Suggested stack

- open public datasets
- map and location context
- local services and place data
- public news or alert feeds

## Feature ideas

- service finder by neighborhood
- map of nearby public resources
- recent public updates with dates
- simple filters for service type and location
- shareable links to a specific service or update

## Example user flow

1. A resident opens the tool and selects a neighborhood.
2. Nearby public services appear on a map and in a list.
3. Recent updates are shown with their source and date.
4. The resident filters by service type or distance.
5. The resident opens one result and shares the link with someone else.

## Why this matters

Civic tools are most useful when they reduce the effort of finding information that is already public. The product does not need to create new data; it needs to connect existing sources around a clear local need.
