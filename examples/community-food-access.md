# Community food access example

## Product idea

A community food map that helps residents find nearby groceries, markets, pantries, and meal programs while showing where access gaps overlap with income and population data.

## Problem it solves

Food resources are often listed in separate directories, and nutrition information is disconnected from the places people actually shop. Residents and organizers need one view that connects availability, cost, and distance.

## Suggested stack

- USDA FoodData Central for nutrition data
- Open Food Facts for product and label data
- US Census ACS for income and population context
- OpenStreetMap for map and distance context
- Overpass or Foursquare for local food resources

## Feature ideas

- map and list views of food resources
- filters for groceries, markets, pantries, and meal sites
- walking or transit distance to the nearest option
- nutrition context for common products
- neighborhood access summaries with source dates

## Example user flow

1. A resident enters an address or selects a neighborhood.
2. Nearby food resources appear on a map and in a list.
3. The resident filters by resource type and distance.
4. Nutrition and product data explain available options.
5. The resident shares a link to the neighborhood resource list.

## Why this matters

Food access is about more than the number of stores. It depends on distance, affordability, and what those places actually offer. This product makes those relationships visible without inventing new data.
