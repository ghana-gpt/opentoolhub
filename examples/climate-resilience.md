# Climate resilience example

## Product idea

A neighborhood climate dashboard that combines historical climate data, current weather alerts, public indicators, and health context to support local adaptation planning.

## Problem it solves

Climate risk is local, but the evidence is scattered across weather services, open data portals, and health agencies. Planners need one place to compare hazards and decide where to invest first.

## Suggested stack

- NASA POWER for historical climate and meteorological context
- National Weather Service for forecasts and active alerts
- World Bank or Data.gov for public indicators
- OpenStreetMap for neighborhoods and critical facilities
- WHO or CDC data for health and community impact

## Feature ideas

- neighborhood heat and flood risk summaries
- current alerts next to historical conditions
- map layers for vulnerable populations and critical facilities
- source dates and geography for every indicator
- exportable briefing for a planning meeting

## Example user flow

1. A planner selects a neighborhood.
2. Historical climate data and current alerts load for the area.
3. Public health and population data add context.
4. The map highlights where hazards overlap with vulnerable residents.
5. The planner exports a short briefing with links to each source.

## Why this matters

Adaptation decisions need to be defensible. This product does not predict the future; it organizes existing evidence so a community can ask better questions and prioritize action.
