# Food access map starter

This starter is for community food maps that connect nutrition information with the places where people actually shop, eat, and receive assistance.

## Typical goal

A nonprofit, public agency, or community group wants to show where affordable and nutritious food is available, where access is thin, and which local resources can help fill the gap.

## Suggested stack

- USDA FoodData Central for nutrition and food composition data
- Open Food Facts for crowd-sourced product and label data
- US Census ACS for neighborhood income and population context
- OpenStreetMap for distance, streets, and map context
- Overpass or Foursquare for groceries, markets, pantries, and meal sites

## Example flow

1. The user selects a neighborhood or enters an address
2. Nearby food resources appear on a map and in a list
3. Nutrition and product data explain what is available
4. Census context shows population and income patterns
5. The user filters by resource type, distance, or opening hours

## Recommended features

- map and list views that stay in sync
- filters for grocery stores, markets, pantries, and meal programs
- walking and transit distance where data is available
- source and last-updated labels on every resource
- shareable links to a neighborhood or resource

## Best use cases

- community food resource guides
- food insecurity research
- nutrition education tools
- local food policy dashboards

## Why this matters

Food access is shaped by distance, cost, and available options. Connecting nutrition data to local places helps communities see the gap and act on it.
