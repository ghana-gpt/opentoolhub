# Climate resilience dashboard starter

This starter is for local teams that need to turn climate and weather data into a clear picture of who and what is at risk.

## Typical goal

A city, county, or community organization wants a dashboard that combines climate hazards, environmental conditions, and public-health context so residents and planners can see where adaptation work matters most.

## Suggested stack

- NASA POWER for historical climate and meteorological context
- National Weather Service for US forecasts and alerts
- World Bank or Data.gov for public indicators and local datasets
- OpenStreetMap for neighborhoods, facilities, and hazard context
- WHO or CDC data for health and community impact context

## Example flow

1. The user selects a neighborhood, city, or region
2. Historical climate and current weather conditions load for that area
3. Public datasets add population, infrastructure, and health context
4. Hazards and vulnerable locations are highlighted on a map
5. The user compares risks and exports a short briefing for planning

## Recommended features

- place-based risk summaries with source dates
- map layers for heat, flooding, and critical facilities
- links from every metric to its provider and dataset
- plain-language explanations of uncertainty
- exportable briefings for local decision-makers

## Best use cases

- local climate adaptation plans
- heat and flood preparedness
- community resilience briefings
- environmental health dashboards

## Why this matters

Climate data is only useful when it is connected to a place and the people who live there. This template keeps the evidence traceable while making the local impact easy to understand.
